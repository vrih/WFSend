How the school in an EHC plan is decided

Children with an EHC plan don't apply for a school place in the usual way. The school is named in section I of the plan.

  1. You get a draft plan. It doesn't name a school yet. You have at least 15 days to comment on it and to ask for a particular school or college to be named ({{ cite("cfa_2014_s38", "section 38(2) and (5)") }}; {{ cite("send_regs_2014", "regulation 13(1)") }}). This can be a mainstream school, a special school, an academy or free school, a college, or an independent special school approved by the government.
  2. The council consults the school you asked for, and any other school it is thinking of naming ({{ cite("cfa_2014_s39", "section 39(2)") }}).
  3. The council must name the school you asked for unless it is unsuitable for your child's age, ability, aptitude or special educational needs, or your child going there would be incompatible with the efficient education of other children or the efficient use of resources ({{ cite("cfa_2014_s39", "section 39(3) and (4)") }}).
  4. If it can't name your choice, it names a school or type of school it thinks is right. That must be a mainstream school unless this goes against your wishes or the efficient education of other children ({{ cite("cfa_2014_s39", "section 39(5)") }}; {{ cite("cfa_2014_s33", "section 33(2)") }}).

The same steps apply when a plan is changed after an annual review, including before your child moves school. See the deadlines for changing school.

Visit the schools you are thinking of and ask whether they can meet your child's needs. SEND in each school lists every school in the borough with the ages and needs each specialist setting takes.

Where children with EHC plans go to school

In Waltham Forest, a larger share of children and young people with a plan are in mainstream schools, and a smaller share in special schools, than in London or England.

If you disagree with the school named

You can appeal to the SEND Tribunal against the school or type of school named in the plan, or against no school being named ({{ cite("cfa_2014_s51", "section 51(2)(c)") }}). If your appeal is only about the school, you don't need a mediation certificate first ({{ cite("cfa_2014_s55", "section 55(2)") }}). See how to appeal. For free advice, contact IPSEA or Waltham Forest SENDIASS.

What these figures don't tell you

  • They can't tell you whether a particular school will have a place for your child. That depends on the school, your child's needs and the council's decision.
  • The forecast and the places can't be set against each other to work out a shortfall. The DfE says the forecasts count children living in the borough, wherever they go to school, while the places are in the borough's schools, whoever fills them.
  • The forecasts are the council's own and may change.
