High needs funding and the schools budget deficit

Support for children with EHC plans is mostly paid for from the High Needs Block of the Dedicated Schools Grant (DSG), a ring-fenced government grant. Spending above the grant builds up as a DSG deficit. A government "statutory override" currently keeps that deficit off the council's main budget, until March 2028 ({{ cite("sf_oct2026", 31) }}).

    The SEND Reform Plan

    Waltham Forest was not in either of the DfE's intervention programmes for councils with deficits, Safety Valve and Delivering Better Value ({{ cite("budget_feb2026", 33) }}). In June 2026, like every council, it had to send the DfE a SEND Reform Plan setting out how it will deliver the national SEND reforms within its budget ({{ cite("sf_jul2026", 177) }}). The DfE accepted the plan on 2 October 2026, and the council now reports to the DfE on it every quarter ({{ cite("sf_oct2026", "pages 30 and 31") }}). On 29 October 2026 the DfE published the High Needs Stability Grant for each council. Waltham Forest's is £12,888,664, 90% of its deficit at 31 March 2026. The grant can only be used to pay off that deficit, not to pay for new services. While the statutory override lasts, the council must hold the grant in a reserve ({{ cite("dfe_hnsg_2026", "grant determination letter, Annex B") }}). The plan's targets are on the targets page, and what we know of the plan so far is on the SEND Reform Plan page.

    We have not found the full plan published. In July the council said a detailed breakdown of the plan's spending, and of its "Experts at Hand" support for schools, would come to the Schools Forum in October 2026 ({{ cite("sf_jul2026", 177) }}). It was not on the October agenda ({{ cite("sf_oct2026", 2) }}).

    What the council's accounts and auditors say

    Most figures above come from reports to the Schools Forum. The council's yearly Statement of Accounts records the same deficit. The council's Audit and Governance Committee also sees its risk register and reports from its auditors.

    How the deficit is shown in the accounts

    The deficit is not charged to the council's main budget, the General Fund, which pays for most council services. Instead the accounts hold it in a separate account, the DSG Adjustment Account. This is one of the council's "unusable reserves" ({{ cite("soa_draft_2026", 96) }}). The statutory override is what allows this. The accounts do not name the override or say when it ends. The council told the Schools Forum it lasts until March 2028 ({{ cite("sf_oct2026", 31) }}).

    What the auditors say

    The council's external auditor is KPMG ({{ cite("soa_draft_2026", 64) }}). Its reports to the committee do not comment on the DSG deficit itself. They raise SEND in two ways.

    • Paying for services in future. For 2024/25, KPMG found a "significant weakness" in the council's arrangements for staying financially sustainable. The council had to apply for £19m of Exceptional Financial Support, special permission from the government to borrow or use other money to balance its 2026/27 budget. KPMG said the council's financial plan shows "rising demand-led pressures across: Temporary Accommodation, Adult Social Care and Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND)" ({{ cite("kpmg_vfm_2026", 5) }}).
    • Value for money of SEND services. In October 2026, KPMG named "Value for Money of SEND arrangements within Children's Services" as a significant risk for 2025/26. It pointed to the 2025 Ofsted and CQC inspection and to an internal audit of the SEN budget. It said there is "a risk that the Council's arrangements for managing and improving SEND services are not operating effectively" ({{ cite("kpmg_vfm_2026", 8) }}). Its work was not finished. It was still waiting for the council's action plan from the inspection ({{ cite("kpmg_vfm_2026", 23) }}). KPMG must report its conclusions by the end of November 2026 ({{ cite("ext_audit_update_oct2026", 1) }}).

    The internal audit of the Special Educational Needs (SEN) budget gave "limited assurance". It made 1 high-risk, 3 medium-risk and 1 low-risk finding ({{ cite("ia_limited_oct2026", 7) }}). The high-risk finding was about EHC plan data. The council's case management system for EHC plans, called MRI, "is not currently being utilised effectively". Teams still rely on spreadsheets kept by hand. In a test of 25 EHC plans, 4 records were missing and 14 had no assessment documents. The system is not linked to the council's finance system. The other findings were: no written procedures for EHC plan processes, no risk management or business continuity plan for the SEN service, a backlog of overdue annual reviews, and some assessments taking longer than the legal limit of 20 weeks ({{ cite("ia_limited_oct2026", 8) }}).

    The audit was part of the 2025/26 internal audit plan, and its scope was set in August 2025. Only its summary is published, so we can't see the full findings, the council's response or its deadlines for acting on them. The test of 25 plans is a sample, not a count of all plans.
