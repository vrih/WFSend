Decisions you can appeal

A parent, or a young person over school-leaving age, can appeal to the SEND Tribunal against these council decisions ({{ cite("cfa_2014_s51", "section 51(2)") }}):

  • not to carry out an EHC needs assessment
  • not to issue an EHC plan after an assessment
  • what a plan says about a child's needs or the support they must get, when the plan is first issued or after it is changed
  • the school or college named in a plan, or that no school is named
  • not to change a plan after an annual review, or not to carry out a re-assessment
  • to stop a plan

If the council refuses an assessment, its letter must tell you that you can appeal, the time limit, and how to get mediation ({{ cite("send_regs_2014", "regulation 5(3)") }}). You can also ask the council to explain its decision and think again, but this does not stop the time limit for appealing.

How to appeal

  1. Contact a mediation adviser. The decision letter tells you who to contact. The adviser explains mediation, which is a meeting with the council and an independent mediator to try to settle the disagreement.
  2. Get a mediation certificate. The adviser must give you one once they have explained mediation, whether you go on to mediation or tell them you don't want to ({{ cite("cfa_2014_s55", "section 55(3) to (5)") }}). You don't need a certificate if your appeal is only about the school or type of school named in the plan, or that no school is named ({{ cite("cfa_2014_s55", "section 55(2)") }}).
  3. Send your appeal to the Tribunal within 2 months of the date of the decision letter, or 1 month after the date of the mediation certificate, whichever is later ({{ cite("tribunal_rules_2008", "rule 20(1)(c)") }}). See GOV.UK: appeal to the SEND Tribunal.

Enter the date of your decision letter in the deadlines calculator to see your dates. Appealing is free. For help with an appeal, contact IPSEA or Waltham Forest's SEND information, advice and support service (SENDIASS).

Appeals and complaints

An appeal is about a decision: whether to assess, whether to issue a plan, or what the plan says. If the council has missed a deadline, not replied or not delivered what a plan says, you can complain to the council and then to the Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman. The Ombudsman usually won't look at decisions you can appeal. See how to complain.
