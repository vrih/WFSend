The steps and the legal deadlines

These steps apply when your child is at a school, nursery or college. The council can ask the head teacher to arrange the review meeting ({{ cite("send_regs_2014", "regulation 20(1)") }}).

  1. The review is due within 12 months of the plan being issued, or of the last review ({{ cite("cfa_2014_s44", "section 44(1)") }}).
  2. You are invited to the meeting with at least 2 weeks' notice. The school, the council's SEND service, health and social care must be invited too ({{ cite("send_regs_2014", "regulation 20(2) and (3)") }}).
  3. You get the reports at least 2 weeks before the meeting. Whoever arranges the meeting must ask everyone invited for advice and information about your child, and send it round ({{ cite("send_regs_2014", "regulation 20(4)") }}).
  4. The meeting looks at your child's progress towards the outcomes in the plan, and whether they are still right. The council must take account of your views and your child's ({{ cite("send_regs_2014", "regulations 19 and 20(5)") }}). From Year 9, it must also cover preparing for adulthood and independent living ({{ cite("send_regs_2014", "regulation 20(6)") }}).
  5. A report of the meeting is sent to everyone invited within 2 weeks, with recommendations for any changes to the plan ({{ cite("send_regs_2014", "regulation 20(7) and (9)") }}).
  6. The council decides: 4 weeks after the meeting. It must tell you whether it will keep the plan as it is, change it or end it ({{ cite("send_regs_2014", "regulation 20(10)") }}).
  7. If it changes the plan, it sends you the proposed changes and the evidence for them. You have at least 15 days to comment and ask for a particular school. The final plan is due within 8 weeks of the proposed changes being sent ({{ cite("send_regs_2014", "regulation 22") }}).

Enter the date of the meeting in the deadlines calculator to see each date.

If your child is changing school

With a deadline in February or March, the review usually needs to happen in the autumn term before, so ask the school when it will be if you haven't heard. The plan must name the new school by the deadline, so you can tell the school and the council early which schools you want to be considered. See special school places and SEND in each school.

If the review is late or you disagree

  • If the review doesn't happen, or the decision is late, you can complain to the council and then to the Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman. See how to complain.
  • If the council decides not to change the plan, or to end it, you can appeal to the SEND Tribunal ({{ cite("cfa_2014_s51", "section 51(2)(e) and (f)") }}). Its decision letter must tell you how ({{ cite("send_regs_2014", "regulation 20(11)") }}).
  • If it changes the plan, you can appeal against what the new plan says, or the school it names, once it is final ({{ cite("cfa_2014_s51", "section 51(3)(b)") }}).

See how to appeal. For free advice, contact IPSEA or Waltham Forest SENDIASS.

What these figures don't tell you

  • The DfE figures cover {{ ar.period }} only, so they can't show whether things are getting better or worse.
  • The council's own monitoring measures reviews done on time, which is not the same as the DfE's measure, so the figures don't match.
  • The council has said data from its case management system is inaccurate ({{ cite(local.data_quality_warning.source, local.data_quality_warning.page) }}).
