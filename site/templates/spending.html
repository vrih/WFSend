Placements in independent special schools

Independent and non-maintained special schools charge fees, which the council pays from the High Needs Block. In June 2026, in answer to a Freedom of Information (FOI) request, the council said it had {{ ip.rows[0]["values"][-1] }} children and young people in these schools in 2025-26, against {{ ip.rows[0]["values"][0] }} in 2022-23. What it spent on them rose from {{ ip.rows[1]["values"][0] }} to {{ ip.rows[1]["values"][-1] }} a year.

New places cost more than the average. For the {{ npl.rows[0].value }} new independent special school places it commissioned in 2025-26, the average annual fee was {{ npl.rows[5].value }} and the median was {{ npl.rows[6].value }}. The council buys each place case by case ("spot purchasing"), not through a framework with agreed prices ({{ cite(npl.source, "pages 1 and 2") }}). The council's SEND Sufficiency Plan puts the average cost of an out-of-borough independent or special school place at about £60,000 a year ({{ cite("sufficiency_plan_2025", 59) }}).

Payments to suppliers

Each month the council publishes every payment of £500 or more it makes to suppliers. These are the payments it coded to its SEND services. They show who the money goes to. They don't cover everything the council spends on SEND: payments under £500 and staff pay aren't listed.

Why only these years: the reports from {{ pay.first_coded_label }} onwards use the council's current finance codes, and earlier reports can't be matched to these services. From April 2026 the reports list far fewer payments for every service, not just SEND: {{ "{:,.0f}".format(pay.after.payments) }} a month on average, against {{ "{:,.0f}".format(pay.before.payments) }} in {{ ly }}. We have left 2026/27 out until the council explains the change.
