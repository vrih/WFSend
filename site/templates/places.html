    New places at Chapel End

    In February 2026, the council announced a new site for children with SEND in the former Chapel End Early Years Centre on Brookscroft Road, Walthamstow. Lime Academy Hornbeam, one of the borough's special schools, runs it as part of its existing school. The council said the site would open to 40 pupils in September 2026 and grow to 100 pupils over the next 3 years ({{ cite("chapel_end_news_feb2026") }}).

    The site opened in September 2026 as the school's Maple Green campus, which the school says provides "specialist primary provision for Autism" ({{ cite("hornbeam_welcome") }}). In its first week, the school said it had "welcomed in our first 40 learners" ({{ cite("hornbeam_newsletter_sep2026", 3) }}).

    The planning application, which went to the council's planning committee in July 2026, is for a school of "up to 40 pupils with 32 staff" ({{ cite("chapel_end_planning_jul2026", 2) }}). The council hasn't said how or when the site would grow to 100 places. These places are not in the table above, which counts places in May {{ ss_year[:2] ~ ss_year[-2:] }}.

    Children with an EHC plan and no school named

    An EHC plan must name the school or college a child will go to, in section I.     Education outside school

    Some children with an EHC plan are taught outside school. With education otherwise than at school (EOTAS), the council arranges and pays for a child's education somewhere other than a school, because school isn't suitable for them. Interim or home tuition is meant to be temporary, while the child waits for a school place.

    Placements outside the borough

    In 2025-26 the council commissioned {{ npl.rows[0].value }} new places in independent special schools and none in non-maintained special schools. It made {{ npl.rows[2].value }} other new placements outside the borough. It said none of the {{ npl.rows[4].value }} placements were in Waltham Forest. It buys these places case by case ("spot purchasing"), not through a framework of approved schools ({{ cite(npl.source, "pages 1 and 2") }}).

    In October 2025 the council told the Health and Wellbeing Board that {% for b in local.backlogs if b.label.startswith("Children placed out of borough") %}{{ b.value }}{% endfor %} children had been placed out of the borough "this year", against 28 the year before ({{ cite("hwb_oct2025_report", 3) }}). The FOI answer counts out-of-area placements "of any type", which may include colleges and mainstream schools in other boroughs, and covers April 2025 to March 2026. Even so, the two figures are far apart, and the council hasn't explained the difference. The same report says that in November 2024, 17% of SEND education placements were outside the borough ({{ cite("hwb_oct2025_report", 2) }}), and the SEND Sufficiency Plan puts it at 12% of children and young people with EHC plans ({{ cite("sufficiency_plan_2025", 28) }}).

    Children out of school

    Some children with SEND leave school because their needs aren't being met. The DfE doesn't publish home education or children missing education by SEN for each council. Across England, 16% of children electively home educated in autumn 2025 were on SEN support and 7% had an EHC plan, compared with 14% and 5% of all pupils (DfE, {{ PUBS.ehe.title }}).
