The DfE's register of schools, Get Information About Schools, records the ages each special school takes and the types of need it caters for. Schools and the council keep these entries up to date themselves.

Some mainstream schools have places set aside for children with a particular type of need. In resourced provision, children are taught mostly in ordinary classes and get extra support. In an SEN unit, children are taught mostly in separate classes. Places are usually for children with an EHC plan.

The DfE says these forecasts can't be set against the capacity figures above to work out a shortfall. The forecasts count children living in the borough in Reception to Year 11, wherever they go to school, including independent and alternative provision. The capacity figures count places in the borough's schools, including sixth forms, whoever fills them. The council doesn't report how many new places it plans.

Children out of school

Some children with SEND leave school because their needs aren't being met. The DfE doesn't publish home education or children missing education by SEN for each council. Across England, 16% of children electively home educated in autumn 2025 were on SEN support and 7% had an EHC plan, compared with 14% and 5% of all pupils (DfE, {{ PUBS.ehe.title }}).
