How are SEND services in Waltham Forest performing?

Parents have asked Waltham Forest Council for clear performance figures and targets for special educational needs and disability (SEND) services. This site brings together what has already been published by the Department for Education, Ofsted and the council's own committee papers. Every figure links to its source.

No published targets. The council's SEND Improvement Plan tracks measures such as 20-week timeliness and annual reviews completed on time. It records baselines but sets no numeric target for either. The SEND Strategy 2025–2028 says "clear targets" will sit in a separate Local Area Plan ({{ cite("send_strategy_2025", 4) }}). We have not found those targets published. See every target we could find.

What changed

For four years, Waltham Forest issued about nine in ten new EHC plans within the 20-week legal limit, well above London and England. That fell to {{ fmt(t.series[WF]["2024"]) }} in 2024 and {{ fmt(t.series[WF]["2025"]) }} in {{ t.latest_period }}. That puts the borough {{ rank_text(t) }}. The council's monthly monitoring shows the same thing: {{ fmt(lt.value) }} in {{ lt.period }}. The council puts this down to delays in educational psychologist advice ({{ cite(lt.source, lt.page) }}).

About the figures

National statistics come from the Department for Education's Explore Education Statistics and are refreshed automatically. Local figures come from council and NHS papers, cited to the page. They are copied as printed, including the council's own warning that its case management data may be inaccurate ({{ cite(local.data_quality_warning.source, local.data_quality_warning.page) }}).
