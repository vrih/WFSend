Free, independent advice

  • Waltham Forest SENDIASS (SEND information, advice and support service) gives free, confidential and impartial advice to parents, carers, children and young people. It is run at arm's length from the council. It can help with EHC needs assessments, EHC plans, annual reviews, choosing a school and disagreements with the council. Call 07494 280 073 or send an enquiry online.
  • IPSEA (Independent Provider of Special Education Advice) is a national charity. It gives free advice based on the law, and has template letters and guides to EHC plans and appeals.
  • Contact is a national charity for families with disabled children. It runs a free helpline.
  • Waltham Forest Parent Forum is a voluntary group of parents and carers of children and young people with SEND aged 0 to 25. It runs meetings and passes parents' views on to the council.

The council's SEND information

  • The Local Offer is the council's guide to SEND services in Waltham Forest. Every council must publish one.
  • Raising disputes and complaints explains who to contact if your SEND officer doesn't reply, and how to complain about the council, a school or the NHS.

If you disagree with the council or want to complain

Which route to use depends on what the problem is. SENDIASS can help you decide.

You disagree with a decision

You can appeal to the SEND Tribunal against many decisions. These include the council refusing to assess, refusing to issue a plan, what a plan says, the school it names, or ending a plan.

Before you appeal, you usually have to contact a mediation adviser. The council's decision letter tells you how. There are strict time limits. Use the deadlines calculator to work out yours.

Something has gone wrong with the service

For problems such as delays, missed deadlines or support in a plan not being provided, complain to the council using its complaints process. If you are not happy with the answer, you can ask for a second stage review.

For a problem with a school, speak to the school's special educational needs coordinator (SENCO) first, then use the school's own complaints procedure.

The council's answer to your complaint has not put things right

You can complain to the Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman. It is free. The Ombudsman usually expects you to finish the council's complaints process first, and to complain within 12 months of knowing about the problem. It normally won't look at a decision you could appeal to the SEND Tribunal. Read its guide to complaints about special educational needs.
